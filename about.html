The Current Role

Leading Graphic Packaging from Atlanta

Robbert Rietbroek is President and Chief Executive Officer of Graphic Packaging Holding Company, a publicly traded paper-based packaging leader (NYSE: GPK) headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia. The company is one of the largest producers of paperboard, folding cartons, and fiber-based consumer-packaging solutions in the world, supplying many of the most recognized food, beverage, household, and foodservice brands. As CEO, Rietbroek owns the strategic agenda, capital allocation, and operating performance of the enterprise.

His brief, in his own framing, is straightforward: keep Graphic Packaging's commercial and operational engine running at a high standard while accelerating the long-term shift toward fiber-based packaging. That dual mandate — disciplined execution today, structural growth tomorrow — runs through everything from plant-network investment decisions to the company's customer-facing innovation roadmap. He has unpacked that mandate in greater depth across his perspective on sustainable paper-based packaging.

The Path In

From inaugural CEO of Primo Brands to Graphic Packaging

Before stepping into the Graphic Packaging seat, Rietbroek served as inaugural Chief Executive Officer and Director of Primo Brands Corporation, the publicly traded North American branded bottled-water leader. He built that company from the ground up: as CEO of Primo Water Corporation, he led the transformative merger with BlueTriton Brands that closed in November 2024 to form Primo Brands, then stood up its operating model, leadership team, and public-company governance from day one.

Standing up a public company from a merger is its own discipline — integration plans, synergy capture, governance, talent reset, all running in parallel under quarterly scrutiny. For Robbert Rietbroek, it was the proving ground for the kind of full-cycle CEO work he now leads at Graphic Packaging. Some of the lessons he carried out of that experience he has written up in a longer reflection on the journey from brand manager to the C-suite.

The Operator's School

P&L leadership at PepsiCo and Kimberly-Clark

Earlier in his career, Rietbroek ran two of the more demanding general-management roles in consumer products. At PepsiCo, he served as Senior Vice President and General Manager of Quaker Foods North America, where he oversaw end-to-end operations and full P&L responsibility — directing brand management, operational excellence, and strategic growth initiatives across one of America's most iconic breakfast portfolios. Before that, he ran PepsiCo Australia and New Zealand as Senior Vice President and General Manager.

The PepsiCo seats followed a parallel run at Kimberly-Clark, where he led the company's Global Baby and Child Care business and earlier served as managing director for Kimberly-Clark Australia & New Zealand. Two decades inside two of the world's most operationally rigorous consumer-products companies left him with the muscle memory that defines a modern CEO: how to read a P&L, how to build a category, and how to translate strategy into plant-floor and shelf-level execution. Robbert covers more of that operating philosophy on his Substack newsletter.

The Foundation

Fifteen years at Procter & Gamble across three continents

Rietbroek began his career at Procter & Gamble, where he stayed for 15 years and held a sequence of roles across Europe, South America, and the United States. The arc was the classic P&G one — strategic initiatives and brand development, with progressively larger remits — but the texture was unusual: by the time he left, he had built brands inside three very different consumer markets, learned to operate inside three different regional cultures, and absorbed the brand-management craft from one of the institutions that effectively invented it.

That foundation shows up in everything he has built since. The boardroom-era career story — the moves, the mandates, the lessons that compound — is laid out in fuller form in a piece tracing his career across continents. For a more visual snapshot of his work and speaking, his Vimeo channel and Robbert Rietbroek's SlideShare archive collect representative material.

Beyond the Day Job

Boards, councils, patents, and education

Beyond the Graphic Packaging mandate, Rietbroek serves on the boards of the Metro Atlanta Chamber and the American Forest and Paper Association — two organizations whose remit overlaps directly with his own industry. He is also a member of the Wall Street Journal CEO Council, and he holds three packaging-design patents, a small but telling reminder that his interest in the category is as much hands-on as strategic.

He holds a master's degree from Maastricht University in his native Netherlands, the institution where the analytical foundation of a 25-year operating career began. Periodic interviews and longer-form profiles — including his Medium archive — offer additional context on how he thinks about leadership, packaging, and the industries he has spent a career inside.
